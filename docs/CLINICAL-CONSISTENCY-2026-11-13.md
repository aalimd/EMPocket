# Clinical consistency audit · 13 November 2026

This pass checked coverage and source mappings across all 58 presentations and
reviewed high-risk overlaps among the topic pages, Quick, bedside tools, ECG
teaching and practice cases. The findings below describe the points verified
against primary guidance and corrected in this release.

- **Medication reference.** The Quick hyperkalemia card now names the UK Kidney
  Association adult regimen, gives the infusion time and links to the
  electrolyte monitoring instructions. The overdose page distinguishes the
  21-hour and SNAP acetylcysteine regimens and records the ACMT 2026 criteria
  for ending IV treatment. Sodium bicarbonate reassessment uses ECG, perfusion,
  pH and sodium.
- **Decision tools.** The Canadian CT Head Rule includes all seven findings.
  That calculator and PERC require confirmation that the rule applies and its
  criteria were assessed. The DVT calculator follows the two-level NICE NG158
  pathway. Quick's PECARN card now opens Head Injury.
- **Pediatric dosing.** The calculator applies the cap for the displayed
  anaphylaxis regimen and the atropine minimum for selected bradycardia.
  Hypoglycemia, meningitis, croup and septic shock rows name their indication
  and follow the linked protocols. The meningitis dose applies from 2 months.
- **Airway and ECG.** Induction guidance explains that ketamine can be
  followed by hypotension. The de Winter fallback caption now directs immediate
  cardiology and reperfusion assessment. The QT calculator labels both
  formulas and directs users to the ECG guide for interpretation.
- **Metabolic and learning content.** The osmolality tool labels the effective
  and total estimates and asks for BUN in mg/dL before showing the total.
  The febrile infant case uses the AAP pathway for the actual age in the
  scenario. Quick recognizes anaphylaxis without skin signs after a likely
  trigger and identifies the RCUK fluid regimen.

New primary sources are linked from the relevant presentation or calculator.
The source date on a presentation describes the selected points reviewed.
The October historical audit remains a separate record. This pass prioritizes
the corrections above. It does not independently validate every clinical
statement, ECG recording label, drug regimen or local protocol.

The app's tests check clinical consistency, calculator boundaries, local
browser behavior and the offline release. An independent clinical review
remains necessary for formal validation as a point-of-care resource.
